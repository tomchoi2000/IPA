CAT-Q

Camouflaging Behaviour

The Camouflaging Autistic Traits Questionnaire (CAT-Q) is a self-report tool designed to measure the extent to which individuals engage in camouflaging behaviors—strategies used to mask or compensate for autistic traits in social situations. These behaviors can include suppressing natural responses, mimicking others, or consciously adjusting one's communication style to fit social expectations.

Originally developed to better understand the experiences of autistic individuals, especially those who may go undiagnosed due to effective camouflaging, the CAT-Q helps researchers explore how social masking impacts mental health, identity, and daily functioning.

In this study, the CAT-Q is being used as part of a broader investigation into camouflaging behaviors in both autistic and non-autistic populations. Your responses will help us gain insight into how common camouflaging is, why people engage in it, and what psychological effects it may have.

Please answer as honestly and thoughtfully as possible—there are no right or wrong answers. All responses are anonymous and used only for academic research purposes.

AQ

Autism Spectrum Quotient

AQ

Autism Spectrum Quotient

The Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ) is a widely used self-assessment tool designed to measure the degree to which an individual shows traits commonly associated with the autism spectrum. Developed by researchers at the Autism Research Centre at the University of Cambridge, the AQ helps identify patterns of thinking, communication, and social interaction that may reflect autistic traits.

This questionnaire is not a diagnostic tool, but it provides insight into where someone may fall on the spectrum of autistic traits. It is commonly used in both clinical and research settings to better understand how these traits vary across the general population.

In the context of this study, the AQ is used alongside other questionnaires to explore how autistic traits relate to behaviors such as social camouflaging. By comparing responses across different groups, we aim to better understand the diversity of human social behavior and how individuals adapt to social environments.

Your responses will be kept anonymous and used solely for academic research purposes. Please answer each question as honestly and accurately as you can—there are no right or wrong answers.
IQ

IQ

Intelligence Quotient

The Intelligence Quotient (IQ) is a general measure of cognitive abilities, including logical reasoning, problem-solving, verbal understanding, and abstract thinking. While the concept of IQ is often associated with formal testing, in research settings like this one, simplified or adapted tools may be used to gain a general sense of cognitive style or capability.

In this study, IQ is included as a variable to explore how cognitive ability may relate to behaviors such as social camouflaging and the expression of autistic traits. It’s important to note that intelligence is a complex and multi-dimensional concept—it cannot be fully captured by a single number or questionnaire. This measure is not intended to label or judge participants, but to support a deeper understanding of how thinking styles might influence social behavior.

All responses are confidential and used strictly for academic research purposes. There are no right or wrong answers—please respond as honestly and thoughtfully as possible.

Welcome to our Psychology Research Project on Camouflaging Behavior

Thank you for visiting our research website. This platform is part of an independent psychology project focused on understanding camouflaging behavior—the ways in which individuals consciously or unconsciously mask, hide, or adapt their true feelings, thoughts, or behaviors in social settings. Camouflaging can be driven by a desire to fit in, avoid judgment, or navigate social norms, and it plays a significant role in how people experience and manage social interactions.

The aim of this study is to explore the psychological patterns, motivations, and impacts of camouflaging across different populations. By collecting anonymous responses through our questionnaire, we hope to contribute valuable insights to the field of behavioral and social psychology. Your participation will help deepen our understanding of this complex and often overlooked aspect of human behavior.

All data collected is confidential and used solely for academic research purposes. Participation is voluntary and greatly appreciated.
